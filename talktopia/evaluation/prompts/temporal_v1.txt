Timing evidence for this Surface5 interaction:
The timestamps are elapsed wall-clock times on a shared, recorded audio timeline.
They include ASR, language-model, and speech-generation waiting time. Each
sentence interval is tied to the PCM delivered to the listener and its ASR text.
An utterance's sentence details describe that same action; do not count them as
additional turns. ASR sentence boundaries and wording may differ from whole-
utterance recognition. Use received speech as evidence, not generated text.
The committed whole-utterance ASR is the message made available to the listening
agent; use it to assess meaning, agreement, and goal attainment. Sentence ASR
provides supporting timing context. Do not treat differences between these two
recognitions as a participant changing their story or contradicting themselves.

Use the intervals to identify pauses, response timing, and overlapping speech.
Commit order can differ from speaking order; compare timestamps when determining
who was speaking or listening. A backchannel can overlap the speaker without
interrupting them or transferring the floor. An acknowledgment alone does not
establish agreement, acceptance of an offer, or completion of a social goal.

Consider timing when it supports an existing scoring dimension, and cite the
relevant intervals in your explanation. Preserve the original seven dimensions
and their score ranges. Do not award a score simply because a backchannel occurs,
or infer rudeness, disinterest, or intent from a processing delay alone. Timing
cannot establish financial gain, goal attainment, or other outcomes without
supporting conversational evidence.
